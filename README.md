## How I think about HR AI

- **Start with the decision, not the tool.** Pick use cases on value, risk, and readiness.
- **Build governance in from the start.** Legal, Privacy, and employee representatives see the design, not just the launch.
- **Humans make consequential employment decisions.** AI informs hiring, pay, and performance. It does not decide them.
- **Adoption is the product.** Training and measurement are part of the plan from day one.

## Selected work

**How it fits together:** the playbook decides what to build and how to govern it. Resolve shows those controls working in code. Both are evaluated, including where they fall short.

### [HR AI Transformation Playbook](https://github.com/ellehelvig/hr-ai-transformation-playbook)

The operating model for an HR AI program, from choosing use cases to proving value. It includes 37 vetted use cases with a prioritization matrix, governance templates for the EU AI Act and US state and city laws, a literacy curriculum, and a [live ROI calculator](https://ellehelvig.github.io/hr-ai-transformation-playbook/08-roi-measurement/dashboard.html) that uses illustrative data. It also includes tested HR tools an AI assistant can call, which keep a human reviewer in every decision, and an evaluation runner that can block a launch.

### [Resolve: PeopleOps Resolution Agent](https://github.com/ellehelvig/peopleops-resolution-agent)

The controls an HR agent needs, built first and without a language model. An employee request, such as parental leave or a relocation, moves from intake to a recommendation with a policy citation, a named human approval, and an audit trail. It passes its 60-case regression suite. A held-out set of 16 realistically phrased requests passes 0 of 16, and that result defines the job a model would have to do. [Try the demo](https://ellehelvig.github.io/peopleops-resolution-agent/); it runs in your browser.
